Privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026. This policy covers the ScanVault app for Android (package uk.jj.scanvault) and this website. ScanVault is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Everything you scan, sign or record in ScanVault is kept on your phone, encrypted. It is never uploaded, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • ScanVault goes online for two things only: translation language packs you agree to download, and a once-a-day check for the newest version number, which you can switch off.
  • Copies leave the app only when you make them: a shared or saved file, a print, a backup, or the optional copy to a folder.

What stays on your phone

ScanVault keeps everything in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Every page picture and the database are encrypted with AES-256 using a key held by Android's Keystore, so a copy taken off the phone cannot be read.

  • Your documents: the original photos, the finished pages, thumbnails, titles, tags, folders and favourites.
  • The text read from your pages, and where each line sits, so you can search and copy it.
  • Marks you add: signatures, text, ticks, stamps and drawings, and the signature and stamp pictures you save.
  • Your code history, if you keep it switched on.
  • Timestamp records: each photo's reference, fingerprint, time and, if you switched GPS on, its position and grid reference, plus the project, name and notes you typed.
  • Your settings. Your fingerprint and PIN are never seen or stored by ScanVault: Android checks them and only says whether they matched.

ScanVault does not ask for your contacts, microphone, call log or your whole photo library. Picking photos uses Android's own photo picker, which only hands over the pictures you choose.

Features that run on the phone

Text reading, language detection, translation (once a pack is downloaded), code scanning, face finding for ID photos and separating you from the background all use Google ML Kit models built into the app. Smart Erase's AI fill uses the MI-GAN model, also built in. None of them upload your pictures. ML Kit's usage reporting is switched off: the route it would use to send reports has been removed from the app, so any reports are dropped on the phone.

When ScanVault goes online

Translation packs. When you first translate into a language, ScanVault asks before downloading that language's pack from Google, about 30 MB, on Wi-Fi only unless you change it. Your text is not sent: translation happens on the phone. Google's servers see a normal download request.

Update check. At most once a day, ScanVault asks GitHub's public releases address for the newest version number. Nothing about you or your scans is sent; GitHub sees a normal web request. Switch it off in Settings, About.

Sharing, backups and copies

These are made only when you ask, and go only where you choose:

  • Shared files. PDFs and pictures made fresh, with no location, phone model or capture time inside them. Android's share sheet passes them to the app you pick, whose own privacy terms then apply. Temporary copies are deleted the next time ScanVault starts.
  • Timestamp photos. The stamp you set up is printed on the picture itself, including the position if you switched GPS on.
  • Backup file. Everything, sealed with AES-256 using a key made from your password (PBKDF2, 600,000 rounds). Without the password it cannot be opened, by anyone.
  • Copy to a folder. Off until you switch it on: a PDF of each new scan saved to one folder you pick. If that folder is synced to a cloud service, that service's terms apply to the copy.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it. Deleted documents wait in the recycle bin for the time you choose (7, 30 or 90 days) and are then erased; ID Vault documents are deleted straight away. Uninstalling ScanVault deletes everything the app holds. Files you shared or saved elsewhere are yours to delete.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands: you can see it in the app, export it and delete it at any time.

Security

Page pictures and the database are encrypted with keys that never leave Android's Keystore. The app lock and ID Vault use Android's own fingerprint, face and PIN check. Screenshots are blocked while the ID Vault is open, and can be blocked everywhere in Settings. Android's own app backup is switched off for ScanVault. No method is perfect: keep your phone locked and your backup password private.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking scripts. GitHub may keep standard web server logs, such as IP addresses, for security, under GitHub's privacy statement. Downloads come from GitHub's servers on the same terms.

Children

ScanVault collects nothing from anyone. It is a general-purpose tool and is not aimed at children.
